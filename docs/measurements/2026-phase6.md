# Phase 6 measurements

Phase 6 ends with one measurement: the hub's storage and load with two spokes, as section 5.13 of [the design of remote spokes](../design/2026-11-23-remote-spokes.md) asks. Decision [0004](../decisions/0004-crds-first-storage-gate.md) keeps the bulky data in CRDs until one of its triggers fires, and [0063](../decisions/0063-the-hub-copies-spoke-records.md) has the hub's copies of its spokes' records measured against them:

- any inventory object above 512 KiB;
- a sustained write rate above 5 per second;
- inventory objects above 10% of etcd's database.

Every figure on this page is kind's: a synthetic load on three kind clusters on one machine. None comes from a real platform. A figure marked **projected** is not measured: it extends a size that was measured per workload, entry or namespace along a straight line, to a limit that a kind node can't reach.

|---|---|---|---|

| The largest presence record | 28 workloads: 3,893 B as an object, 0.7% of 512 KiB; 1,544 B compressed, 0.2% of the evidence store's cap | measured | 512 KiB |
| A presence record at 512 KiB | about 8,500 workloads of one image in one cluster; the store's cap at about 17,100 | **projected** | 512 KiB |

## Setup

**The clusters.** `make measure-6` sets up three kind clusters of their own with the e2e tests' setup, beside the e2e's local registry, each with one node and a kubeconfig of its own in `bin/`:

- the hub, which runs `test/e2e/config-hub`: `config/hub`, with the vulnerability database in the matcher's image, the scanner's mirror of the local registry, and the registry's login in the scanner's `adminPullSecrets`;
- two spokes, which run `test/e2e/config-spoke` as `cluster-a` and as `cluster-b` (`hack/measure-6/config-spoke-b`).

Their nodes run up to 200 pods (`hack/measure-6/kind-config.yaml`), where kind's default is 110: with the load, a spoke runs 112 pods, and up to 50 more while it rolls.

**The load.** `hack/measure-6` builds 50 images, `img-00` to `img-49`: the e2e's planted image `installed-not-linked`, Alpine 3.12 with the Brotli and the XML library, each with one more file of its own. So each image has a digest of its own, and the findings of that image: 106 open ones, in its sheet of the default playbook. Each spoke runs 100 Deployments of one replica over 30 of the images in 10 namespaces, and 10 of the images, `img-20` to `img-29`, run on both spokes:

| Images of a spoke | Workloads of each | Namespaces of each |
|---|---|---|
| `img-20`, on both spokes | 28 | 10 |
| the other 29 | 1 to 4 | 1 to 4, varied apart from the workloads |

The counts vary apart so that a fit can tell what a workload and a namespace each add to a record. A pod runs `sleep`. Besides the load, each spoke runs 11 images of its own, kind's and vexplain's, which the hub copies too: they are in the totals below, and not in the fits.

**The run.** The tool, on the three clusters' kubeconfigs:

1. adds the spokes to the hub as [the guide](../remote-spokes.md) does: a token of the hub's account on each spoke, the Secret with the spoke's address, CA and token on the hub, and the `Spoke`;
2. pushes the images, deploys the load, and waits until the hub holds it: an entry of each cluster in the `ImageInventory` of each image it runs, the copy of each presence record with all its workloads, the facts of each image's scan, and a sheet for each manifest. It then waits until the hub's writes stand still: at most 2 in a minute;
3. measures what the hub holds, the scans, and the hub manager's metrics;
4. counts the hub's writes for 5 minutes in which nothing changes;
5. rolls out 50 workloads of `cluster-a`, every second one of its load, which touches 23 of its 30 images, by a new annotation on their pod templates, and counts the writes until they stand still again;
6. deletes the hub's manager pod, measures the time until each `Spoke` is `Synced` again, and counts the writes until they stand still.

**How it measures.**

- **Sizes:** the length of an object as `json.Marshal` encodes it, managed fields included, as the operator's own metrics measure it (`vexplain_evidence_object_bytes`). For a presence record also its data compressed, as the evidence store keeps it and its cap counts it, and decoded.
- **Writes:** the hub API server's count of its writes to etcd of the inventory group's objects, `etcd_requests_total` with the operations `create`, `update` and `delete`, sampled every 5 seconds. A record that didn't change isn't written. A phase that the tool ends once the writes stand still took until its last write: the minute without writes that ends it is the tool's wait. The busiest minute is the busiest stretch between two samples at least a minute apart.
- **etcd:** the size of the database file, `apiserver_storage_size_bytes`, and etcd's own `etcd_mvcc_db_total_size_in_use_in_bytes`.
- **Series:** one scrape of the hub manager's metrics endpoint.
- **Memory and CPU:** each pod's cgroup on the hub's node, read every second: `memory.peak`, the most that the pod used since it started, `memory.current`, and the CPU time of `cpu.stat`.
- **Scans:** the scan Jobs' events, the scanner's own metrics, and the scan pods' cgroups. A Job's events don't time a scan: the scanner deletes the Job once it has read the result, often before the Job's controller tells of its completion.
- **The time to `Synced`:** the tool looks at the `Spoke`s five times a second after the deletion, and reads when the new manager took the leader's lease.

**The machine and the run.** A laptop with an Apple M4 Pro of 14 cores and 48 GiB of memory, on macOS 26.5.1, with Docker 29.4 in a Linux VM of 14 CPUs and 15.7 GiB, and kind v0.32.0 with Kubernetes v1.36.1 and containerd 2.3.1. The images were built from commit `e91c91d`, with the vulnerability database `v6.1.10+2026-11-06T06:21:59Z` in the matcher's image. `make measure-6` took 17m39s, setup and cleanup included, of which the tool took 10m33s. Another e2e run shared the machine during it. The run before it, of the same tool but for the timing of the scans, was alone on the machine: it measured the same sizes, to the byte for the sheets and the `ImageInventory` fit and within a few bytes for presence, and the times and writes that are given in brackets below.

**Four figures that the tool now prints.** In the run of this page they were read from the hub with kubectl: the managed fields of the largest `ImageInventory` and the bytes of a workload and of a namespace that it lists, as the length of the JSON that `kubectl get --show-managed-fields -o json` returns, and the sheets' counts of findings and the version of their database. The tool prints them since, and counts the requests that write by verb. A later run of `make measure-6`, at commit `5164fcf` and alone on the machine, printed the same four figures, and the requests by verb that the section on writes gives.

**To repeat it:** `make measure-6` (it needs kind, docker and kubectl). A run that ends well removes its clusters, and one that fails leaves them for a look, until `make cleanup-measure-6`. It shares the e2e tests' registry, which the cleanup of the last run that uses it removes, and the machine: run it alone for times that compare. An e2e run under other cluster names than the defaults keeps the registry from this cleanup only when `make cleanup-measure-6` is given the same `KIND_CLUSTER` and `KIND_SPOKE_CLUSTER`. `make deploy` edits three `kustomization.yaml` in place: put them back before committing.

| presence, of each image in each cluster | 82 | 192,053 | 3,893 |
| exposure, of each workload | 222 | 453,892 | 2,182 |

| image contents, of each image | 50 | 159,998 | 3,201 |

- The largest object is a sheet, 7.7% of 512 KiB. A sheet belongs to a manifest, so the spokes don't make it larger: the hub judges an image once, for all the clusters that run it.
- What grows with the fleet is the copies: a presence record for each image in each cluster, an exposure record for each workload, a record of each namespace's labels, and an entry for each cluster in each image's `ImageInventory`.

### Presence records

A presence record holds an image's workloads in one cluster, each with its pods and their containers. Over the 60 records of the load's images, of 1 to 28 workloads, each workload with one pod of one container, the bytes fit a straight line:

| Bytes of a record | Fixed | Per workload | Largest residual | Limit | Reached at, **projected** |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| As an object | 2,186 | 61.3 | 27 | 0004's 512 KiB | 8,517 workloads |
| Compressed, as the store keeps it | 266 | 45.9 | 19 | the store's cap, 768 KiB | 17,128 workloads |
| Decoded | 92 | 366.8 | 2 | 64 MiB before compression | 182,942 workloads |

- **The object reaches 512 KiB first,** at about 8,500 workloads of one image in one cluster, projected. Its data is base64, a third larger than the compressed bytes, so the store's cap follows at about 17,100.
- The load's workloads have short names, one pod and one container, and no owner labels. A workload lists up to 16 pods, and longer names, more pods, more containers and owner labels make each workload larger, so the limits come sooner.

### ImageInventory

An `ImageInventory` holds an image's facts and an entry for each cluster that runs it. An entry lists up to 100 of the image's workloads in the cluster, and up to `spec.presence.maxNamespaces` of its namespaces, 100 by default and 1,000 at most. Over the 50 objects of the load's images, with 1 or 2 entries of 1 to 28 workloads in 1 to 10 namespaces, the bytes fit a straight line to within 0.4 B:

| Term | Bytes |
|---|---|
| Fixed: the object, and the image's facts | 2,054 |
| An entry | 598 |
| A workload that an entry lists | 236 |
| A namespace that an entry lists | 238 |

- **Managed fields are most of it.** They are the API server's record of the fields that the hub's manager wrote, which names each item of a list. They are 12,941 B of the largest object's 21,232 B, 61%. A listed workload is 85 B of JSON and a listed namespace 93 B: their managed fields add the rest of the 236 and 238 B.
- The store bounds an entry at 256 KiB of its own JSON, without its managed fields. **Projected,** an entry of 100 workloads in 1,000 namespaces stays below that bound, at about 100 KiB of its own JSON, but takes 262,204 B of the object.

**Projected,** an image's object reaches 512 KiB, and etcd's default limit of a request, 1.5 MiB, past which the hub can't write it at all, at these numbers of clusters that run it:

| Each cluster's entry | Bytes of an entry | 512 KiB at | 1.5 MiB at |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 workload in 1 namespace | 1,072 | 488 clusters | 1,466 clusters |
| 28 workloads in 10 namespaces, as `img-20` | 9,589 | 55 | 164 |
| 100 workloads in 100 namespaces, the store's default bounds | 48,009 | 11 | 33 |
| 100 workloads in 1,000 namespaces, the most that an entry lists | 262,204 | 2 | 6 |

An entry lists at most 100 workloads, and at most the bound of namespaces, so the last two rows bound the entry of an image that runs widely in a cluster. The schema allows 256 entries.

### etcd

The hub's etcd file was 22,806,528 B, nearly all of it in use, and the inventory group's objects took 3,662,510 B of it: 16.1% [18.2% of 20,099,072 B]. kind's etcd holds little else, so the share says nothing of a real hub's. What carries over are the bytes per image, per workload and per cluster above, against a real hub's etcd.

## Load

### Writes

The hub writes a record to etcd only when it changes. Its writes of the inventory group's objects, where a phase took until its last write, and idle and the restart, which wrote nothing, the time that the tool counted:

| Phase | Writes | Took | On average | Busiest 10 s | Busiest minute |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| The load's arrival: 200 workloads and 50 new images | 1,551 [1,506] | 1m38s [1m22s] | 15.8/s [18.4] | 50.9/s [58.3] | 22.3/s [22.8] |
| Idle | 0 [0] | 5m01s [4m56s] | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| A rollout of 50 workloads of `cluster-a` | 142 [118] | 26s [30s] | 5.5/s [3.9] | 10.8/s [8.1] | 2.3/s [1.9] |
| A restart of the hub's manager | 0 [0] | 1m02s [1m01s] | 0 | 0 | 0 |

- **The arrival** passes 5 per second by far in its first 40 s [35 s], at up to 56 per second over 5 s, and then writes 4 to 6.5 per second [4.5 to 6], about the trigger, until its last write. It wrote 773 times to `ImageInventory` objects, as entries, workload counts, facts and summaries arrived one after the other, 583 times to `Evidence`, 139 to sheets and 56 to the playbooks' cluster status: about 31 writes for each new image.
- **The rollout** passes 5 per second over the 26 s [30 s] in which its writes came, at 5.5 per second [3.9], and stays below it over a minute, at 2.3 [1.9]. It changed the pods, and so the presence, of 23 images. It wrote 83 times to `Evidence` objects, and 59 times to `ImageInventory` objects, though their entries list workloads and namespaces but no pods: 2.8 writes for each rolled workload [2.4]. The 50 Deployments rolled within 20 s.
- **Records that come and go.** A new pod's container has no image ID until it starts, and until then the spoke keys the image by the reference that the pod names (`ReferenceDigest` in `sdk/workloads/digest.go`): an image of a digest of its own, which the hub copies and deletes once the pod reports the image's digest. The later run counted the API server's requests that wrote, by verb:
  - the arrival created 100 `ImageInventory` objects and deleted 50, where 50 stayed, one for each of the load's images: 50 came and went, as many as the images' references. It created 462 `Evidence` objects and deleted 60, as many as those references' presence in each spoke that ran them, 30 each;
  - the rollout created and deleted 5 `ImageInventory` objects, which came and went, and wrote statuses 20 times: 30 requests. It created and deleted 6 `Evidence` objects, and updated 57: 69 requests.

  For every kind but `ImageInventory`, the requests add up to etcd's writes, to the write. For `ImageInventory` objects etcd counted more: 805 writes in the arrival against 775 requests, and 35 against 30 in the rollout. The API server tries a write to etcd again when the object changed since it read it, and etcd's count takes each try; several of the hub's controllers write an `ImageInventory`'s status, so such tries are the likely rest, which the run doesn't count. How many images come and go depends on how soon the new pods report their digests: a pod that does before the spoke next builds its presence, at most once a second, makes none. **Derived, not measured:** if all of a rollout's writes to `ImageInventory` objects were for such images, at the later run's 35 for 5, the 59 [47] of the rollout above would be about 8 [7] of them. That run didn't count the requests by verb.
- **Projected,** at 2.8 writes for each rolled workload, rolling about 105 workloads within a minute would pass 5 per second over that minute. It may take more: the spoke rebuilds presence at most once a second, so changes that come together share a write.
- **Idle and after a restart,** the hub writes nothing. The new manager's copy finds every record as it was.

### The series of vexplain_open_case

The hub's manager served 11,200 series of `vexplain_open_case`, 5,600 for each spoke: 56 for each of the 200 workloads, from the 106 open findings of its image. A series stands for a CVE with its labels, so findings of one CVE in several packages share one when they agree. The series took 3,666,000 B of a scrape of 3,844,524 B, 95%: 327 B a series, and about 18 KB of every scrape for each workload of these images.

The series grow with the workloads times the open findings of their images, in every cluster, on the hub's one endpoint. This is none of 0004's triggers, but Prometheus reads the whole endpoint at every scrape.

### The manager's memory

The hub's manager used at most 132.0 MiB [132.6] over the load, the idle time and the rollout, and 108.5 MiB at the rollout's end. After its restart, the new one used at most 120.5 MiB [115.6] in its first minute, when it evaluates every manifest again. Its limit is 512 MiB, with `GOMEMLIMIT` at 400 MiB.

### The scans

- **Once for the fleet:** the hub ran one scan Job for each of the 50 images, also for the 10 that both spokes run. It made them 2 s to 85 s after the load's deployment, the median at 50 s, at most 4 at a time by the scanner's default. It ran 6 more for images that kind imported into the spokes' nodes, which no registry serves, and the scanner counted them as `Unauthorized`.
- **How long:** the scanner held a slot for 4.7 s on average [4.1 s], every scan within 10 s. A scan pod lived 2 s to 7 s on the node, the median 4 s [4 s].
- **What the Jobs use:** a scan pod used at most 147 to 244 MiB of memory, the median 160.5 MiB [151 to 229 MiB, the median 160.7], of its 2 GiB request and limit, and 0.85 to 1.43 s of CPU, the median 0.99 s [0.81 to 1.13 s, the median 0.95].
- The hub held the whole load, copied, scanned and judged, 1m34s [1m19s] after the load's deployment.

### The time to Synced after a restart

After the deletion of the hub's manager pod, the new one started within the same second, then waited for the old one's lease, which a manager doesn't release when it stops, and which lasts 15 s: it took it after 18.0 s [16.6 s]. Both `Spoke`s were `Synced` 0.8 s [0.8 s] after that: the copier had synced its caches of both spokes and found nothing to write. A restart costs the lease, and the copy less than a second at this size.

## Against 0004's triggers

- **An object above 512 KiB: none of the records that the spokes add, at this scale.** The largest object here is a sheet, at 7.7%. Sheets don't grow with the spokes, but those of large images pass 512 KiB already, whatever the spokes: phase 2a measured one of 752,744 B, 143% of it. Of the records that the spokes add, the first to reach it is an image's `ImageInventory`, as design 5.13 expected: projected at 11 clusters that run the image with entries at the store's default bounds, and at 2 with entries of 1,000 namespaces. Managed fields are 61% of such an object. A presence record reaches it at about 8,500 workloads of one image in one cluster, projected.
- **A sustained write rate above 5 per second: while much arrives at once.** The hub's writes follow what changes in the fleet: none idle and none after a restart. A rollout of 50 workloads wrote 5.5 per second over the 26 s of its writes, and 2.3 over its busiest minute. In a later run, a rollout's requests created and deleted 5 `ImageInventory` objects and 6 `Evidence` objects, likely the images of new pods' references that came and went. The arrival of 200 workloads and 50 new images wrote up to 56 per second in its first 40 s, then about 5 per second until its last write at 1m38s, and 22.3 over its busiest minute. Whether a real fleet sustains it depends on its rate of pod changes and of new images, which kind can't tell. 0004 waits for a real cluster's week for that.
- **Inventory above 10% of etcd: not measurable on kind.** It is 16.1% here, of an etcd that holds little else. The bytes per image, per workload and per cluster above, against a real hub's etcd, answer it.
- **Outside 0004:** the hub's metrics grow with the workloads times their open findings: 56 series and about 18 KB of every scrape for each workload of these images.

## What it doesn't show

- **A real fleet.** Two spokes of 100 workloads each, with short names, one pod of one container each, and no owner labels.
- **Images as found in practice.** The load's images are small and alike, each with 106 findings. Larger images make larger sheets, SBOMs and scans: phase 2a measured sheets of up to 752,744 B ([2026-phase2a.md](2026-phase2a.md)), and they don't depend on the spokes.
- **A real cluster's churn** over a day: pods that come and go, nodes that drain, larger rollouts.
- **The matcher's database updates,** which change the findings, and with them the sheets, of many images at once. The matcher here holds the database of its image, which it never updates.
- **Spokes across a network.** kind's network is local, so its latency doesn't show.
- **The spokes' own load,** and that of the hub's other pods, such as the matcher.
- **The projected figures** hold only as far as the bytes keep growing as they did in the load.
